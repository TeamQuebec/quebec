Quebec · ICSC 2026 Track B · design review

Your palette is better than you think. It just isn't enforced.

I read the token file and every component that consumes it. The gold-on-near-black identity is distinctive and worth keeping — most identity products in this track will be blue, and a judge who has seen thirty of them will remember yours. The problem is not the hue. It is that six specific things in the system do not do what the names say they do, and three of them are measurable accessibility failures.

What I measured

Every number below is computed from the hex values in app/globals.css with the WCAG 2.1 relative-luminance formula, not estimated. Thresholds: 4.5:1 for body text, 3:1 for large text and for non-text UI such as focus rings.

Breaks

The focus ring is invisible

--ring: #dcc76a against the white card it sits on measures 1.69:1. Non-text contrast needs 3:1 minimum. Every keyboard user in your demo — and a judge tabbing through the verify form will be one — cannot see where they are.

The brand ramp collapses to a single colour

brand-800, brand-900 and brand-950 are all #171717. So every "gradient" built from them is a flat black fill — the receipt header band, the how-it-works panel, the verify console plate. The system looks like it has a ramp and doesn't.

Misses AA

Accent text fails on small type

text-gold-strong (#a5760a) measures 4.04:1 on white and 3.66:1 on its own gold-soft fill. It is used at text-xs for the "Done" tick and the "See how it works" link. gold-deep (#8a6300) already exists in the same file and passes at 4.92:1 — the fix is one token.

The no_match verdict is the only place in the app that reaches for Tailwind's slate family — #f8fafc, #f1f5f9, #64748b. Those are blue-greys sitting on a yellow-biased off-white. Nobody will name it, but it reads as slightly dirty.

The hero grid is drawn with #1a2f47 — a navy. And the entire landing page reads tokens named --af-*, from the template it was adapted from. Neither is visible as a bug; both are the kind of thing that makes a system feel like it drifted.

The rest of the score

Colour is maybe a third of a UI/UX mark. These are the things I found reading the flow, ranked by how much they cost you.

01

The verify button sits below the fold on the one screen that is the product

Before the primary action there is a context banner, three step badges, the reference field with two lines of hint and three example chips, a divider, a section header, and a two-column grid of four selectable checks. On a laptop viewport the button lands under roughly 700px of content. A judge handed a laptop has to scroll to discover the demo's main action.

Fix: make the action bar sticky to the bottom of the card once a reference and one check are present, and show the running selection in it — Verify 2 facts. That also removes the toast you currently fire when someone presses Verify with nothing selected.

02

The landing page and the portal look like two products

The landing page runs on --af-* tokens with a near-black hero; the portals run on brand-* and gold-*. A judge who lands, reads, then enters the portal experiences a seam exactly where you want confidence to be highest.

Fix: collapse the two token sets into one. It is a rename plus a find-and-replace, and it is the highest ratio of perceived polish to effort on this list.

03

Focus is unstyleable because it is unreadable

Beyond the ring colour: the check cards in the verifier are <button> elements wrapping a Checkbox whose pointer events are disabled. They are reachable and operable by keyboard, but with a 1.69:1 ring the state is invisible.

Fix: darken --ring to the accent's -deep step and add focus-visible:ring-2 ring-offset-2 to the card, not just the inner box.

04

Verdicts are colour-coded but never spelled out

Six verdict states resolve to four accent styles, and no_match shares accent: "no" with a genuine NO. A "no identity found" is a very different event from "we found them and the answer is no" — one means check your typing, the other means the fact is false.

Fix: give no_match its own neutral register (the warm grey above) and lead each verdict with its plain-language sentence, not the all-caps label.

05

The receipt prints, but it prints without a title block

Print isolation works — no-print and print-area are applied correctly, and the portal shell is now excluded. But a PDF of a receipt has no page title, no URL, and no "printed on" line, so a saved copy circulates with no way to tell where it came from or when it was made.

Fix: a print-only footer with the receipt URL, the verification timestamp and the vault key fingerprint. It costs nothing on screen and makes the artifact defensible on paper.
